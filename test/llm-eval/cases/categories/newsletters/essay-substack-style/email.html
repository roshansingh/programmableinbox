On slow software

Field Notes · by Maren Holt

For most of my career I believed that fast software was good software. Latency budgets, frame rates, time-to-first-byte: the vocabulary of quality was the vocabulary of speed, and I spent years shaving milliseconds off things nobody had complained about.

What changed my mind was a bakery. The owner had replaced her ordering app with a paper notebook, and when I asked why, she said the app was fast but the notebook was fun. Customers lingered. They chatted. They remembered each other's orders. The friction was the point, and the friction was doing work the app had quietly deleted.

Software teams rarely ask what a delay is doing for the person waiting. A confirmation dialog can be an annoyance, or it can be the one moment someone reconsiders a mistake. A loading screen can be dead time, or it can be the pause that makes a result feel earned. We measure the cost of slowness precisely and the value of it hardly at all.

None of this is an argument for making things worse on purpose. It is an argument for noticing. Before you optimise a step away, spend a day watching what people do with it. Some of it is waste, and you will be glad to find it. Some of it is the reason the thing works, and you will only see that once it has gone.
